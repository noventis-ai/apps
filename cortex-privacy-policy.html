Privacy Policy

What Cortex does with your information

Noventis Cortex is a phone automation app developed and published by Noventis Pvt Ltd — you write rules of the form when this happens, do that, and the app carries them out on your device. This policy is short because there is very little to say: the app has no server, no account and no telemetry, so most of what a privacy policy usually documents does not exist here.

01

The short version

  • Cortex does not collect your personal data.
  • Cortex does not send your data to us or to anyone else.
  • We operate no server that holds Cortex user data, because the app transmits none.
  • Everything Cortex knows about you is stored, encrypted, on your own device.
  • A rule that watches for a message or a call examines it on the device and then discards it. Cortex keeps no copy of your inbox and no copy of your call history.
  • The app makes exactly one kind of network request: downloading a language model file that you choose yourself. It carries no information about you.

Every statement here is checkable. Install Cortex, download a model, then turn on airplane mode. Everything except Model Manager keeps working, because nothing except Model Manager uses the network.

02

Information we collect

None.

We collect no personal information, no usage statistics, no analytics, no crash reports, no device fingerprints and no advertising identifiers. Cortex contains no advertising and no third-party tracking or attribution software.

There is no Noventis account, no sign-in, no profile and no server-side storage associated with the app. We cannot identify you as a user of Cortex, because nothing about your use of it ever reaches us.

03

Information the app processes on your device

Cortex works with a good deal of personal information — it simply never leaves the device it is on. Depending on which features you use and which permissions you grant, the app may read and store, locally:

  • What you type or say to it, and the replies produced on the device
  • Documents you photograph, scan, import or share into the app, and the text extracted from them
  • Dates, amounts and reference numbers found in those documents
  • Reminders you create, or that the app creates from a document
  • The automation rules you write — their triggers, their filters and their actions
  • An activity log of the rules that have run: which rule, at what time, and whether it succeeded
  • Memory entries — contacts you mention, places, preferences, routines, conversation summaries
  • Contacts, calendar entries, files, photos and device settings that a skill you invoke reads
  • Audio captured while you are speaking to it, processed by Android's on-device speech recognition

All of this is held in an encrypted database on your device. The encryption key is generated on the device and kept in the Android Keystore, so the database is readable only on that device. The app also disables Android's automatic cloud backup for its own data, so the operating system does not copy the database off the device either.

We have no access to any of it, at any point, by any means.

04

Messages and calls

Two of the triggers you can put in a rule concern messages and calls. They are the only reason Cortex asks for the corresponding permissions, and this section describes exactly what happens when one of them is granted.

A message arriving. With Receive SMS granted, Android hands Cortex each message at the moment it arrives. Cortex compares it against the filters in your own rules — a sender you named, a word you chose. If nothing matches, the message is discarded there and then and nothing about it is written down. If a rule matches, Cortex runs the actions you set for it and adds one line to the activity log recording which rule ran, when, and whether it succeeded.

Cortex cannot read the messages already on your phone. It does not hold the permission that opens the message store. Messages that arrived before you wrote a rule are invisible to it, and no copy of your inbox is made at any point.

Cortex cannot send messages. It does not hold the send-SMS permission either. A rule that composes a message opens your own messaging app with the text filled in, and you decide whether to send it.

A call ending. With Phone state and Read call log granted, Android tells Cortex when a call finishes and Cortex reads that one call's entry — the number, and whether it was missed — to decide whether a call-based rule of yours applies. It is read at the moment the rule is evaluated and not retained. Cortex keeps no copy of your call history.

What persists. Two things, both on your device and both yours: the rules you wrote, and the log of the runs they produced. Both are listed in the Automations screen and can be deleted there, individually or entirely.

None of it is transmitted. No message, no number, no call and no rule leaves your phone. There is nowhere for them to go: the app has no server to send them to.

05

Where the language model runs

On your phone.

Cortex loads a quantised language model file stored on the device and runs it on the device's own processor. Your requests are interpreted, planned and answered locally. Nothing you type or say is transmitted to Noventis, to a model provider, or to any third party in order to be processed.

Cortex is software you run, not a service we operate on your behalf. We do not see your requests and could not retrieve them if we were asked to.

06

The one time Cortex uses the internet

Cortex requests network access for a single purpose: downloading a model file when you choose one in Model Manager and tap Download. Nothing else in the app makes a network request.

That download is an ordinary HTTPS file fetch from the public host listed for that model, performed by Android's system download manager. It includes no information about you, your device's contents, or anything you have said to Cortex. As with any download from any host, the server providing the file necessarily sees the network address it is sending the file to; that is a property of how the internet works, not information the app supplies, and we receive none of it.

Those model files are published by their respective authors under their own licences and terms. We do not license them to you and make no representation about them.

You may skip the download entirely and supply your own model file. Once a model is installed, Cortex is fully functional with the network switched off.

07

Sharing and disclosure

We share nothing, because we hold nothing.

We do not sell, rent, trade or disclose personal information to third parties. There is no third party with access to your Cortex data. There is no dataset of Cortex users for a future acquirer, court order or law-enforcement request to reach — a request of that kind would be answered by saying that no such data exists.

Actions you ask Cortex to perform may hand information to other apps on your phone — sending a message passes your text to your messaging app, opening a location passes it to your maps app. Those apps are governed by their own privacy policies, and Cortex asks you to confirm before any action that leaves the device in this way.

09

Your data, and your control over it

Everything Cortex remembers about you is visible in the Memory screen, where each entry can be inspected, pinned, edited or deleted individually. What Cortex is permitted to learn is governed by switches in Settings.

Documents you capture or import are listed in the Documents screen and can be deleted individually. Cortex records medical, identity and financial documents by category only — noting that such a document exists, never storing its contents — regardless of your other settings.

The rules you have written are listed in the Automations screen, where each can be paused, edited or deleted. Each rule carries its own run history — the log described in section 04 — which is deleted with it, and can be cleared on its own at any time.

You may export your data at any time from within the app.

Deleting everything. Clearing the app's storage in Android settings, or uninstalling Cortex, destroys the encrypted database and the key material that reads it. Neither is recoverable afterwards — by you, or by us. There is no separate deletion request to make and no account for us to close, because we hold nothing to delete. If you would like written confirmation of that, write to support@noventis.ai and we will provide it.

Device security is yours. Cortex encrypts its own storage, but a device that other people can unlock is a device on which your assistant can be read.

10

Children

Cortex is not directed at children and is not intended for use by anyone under 18. We do not knowingly collect information from children — or from anyone else, as section 02 explains.

11

Legal rights

Privacy laws including the GDPR, the CCPA and India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act give you rights to access, correct, delete and port your personal data, and to object to its processing.

Those rights are exercised against whoever holds and processes your data. For Cortex, that is you: your data lives on your device, and the app gives you direct access, correction, deletion and export for all of it, without asking anyone's permission. Noventis is not a data controller or processor of your Cortex content, because we never receive it.

If you believe we hold data about you and would like to exercise a right over it, write to support@noventis.ai.

12

Security

Cortex stores its data in a SQLCipher-encrypted database. The key is generated on your device and held in the Android Keystore, backed by hardware-secured storage where the device provides it. Automatic cloud backup of the app's data is disabled. The app maintains a tamper-evident local audit log of the actions it takes on your behalf.

No security measure is absolute. A device that is unlocked, rooted or compromised is one on which these protections can be defeated.

13

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy we will update the effective date above, publish the new version at this address, and ship the updated text inside the app. Material changes will be described in the release notes for the version that carries them.

Because Cortex holds no account and stores nothing about you outside your own device, uninstalling the app ends the relationship completely. There is nothing left with us to close.
